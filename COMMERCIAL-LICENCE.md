## What you may do without asking

The licence permits any noncommercial purpose. In practice that covers:

- Reading the code, running it locally, testing it, learning from it.
- Hobby, personal and amateur use, and self-hosting it for yourself.
- Forking it and publishing your changes, as long as your use stays
  noncommercial and you pass on the licence and the required notice.
- Use by a charitable organisation, an educational institution, a public
  research organisation, a public safety or health organisation, an
  environmental protection organisation, or a government institution. That
  holds regardless of how the organisation is funded.

A not-for-profit disability support provider running Vigilo for its own
participants is inside the licence and owes nothing.

## What needs written permission first

Anything commercial. Including, and not limited to:

- Running Vigilo, or anything derived from it, in a for-profit business, even
  purely internally and even if you never charge anyone for it.
- Selling it, sublicensing it, or bundling it into a product or service you
  sell.
- Hosting it for other people as a service, paid or free, where the hosting
  supports a commercial operation.
- Using it to deliver services you are paid for, or to support billing,
  contracts or funded packages.
- Consulting, implementation, support or training offered for a fee, where the
  work is on Vigilo or a derivative of it.
- Using it in a product demonstration, tender, pitch or investor material for a
  commercial venture.

If it makes money, supports making money, or happens inside a for-profit
entity, assume it needs a licence and ask.

## Asking

1. Who you are, and the legal entity that would hold the licence.
2. What you want to do with Vigilo, and roughly at what scale (how many
   participants, how many staff, one instance or many).
3. Whether you intend to modify it, and whether you intend to redistribute it.
4. Whether it is self-hosted by you or hosted for someone else.

Permission is granted case by case, in writing, to a named entity. Terms are
whatever the written grant says. Nothing on this page is itself an offer or a
licence, and no other document, README line, blog post, conversation or issue
comment grants commercial rights.

## Scope of a grant

Unless the written grant says otherwise:

- It covers only the entity named in it, not parents, subsidiaries or
  affiliates.
- It is not transferable, and it does not survive an acquisition of that
  entity.
- It does not permit sublicensing or redistribution.
- It grants no ownership of Vigilo, and no rights to the Vigilo name.
